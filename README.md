# Human–AI Contribution Provenance

> Working name: **HACP**  
> Status: **Experimental Draft v0.2**

Human–AI Contribution Provenance is an experimental, vendor-neutral specification for describing **how humans and AI systems contributed to digital content**.

HACP does not try to answer only:

> “Was this generated by AI?”

Instead, it describes the actual creation workflow:

- who contributed,
- which AI systems were involved,
- what each actor contributed,
- in which order contributions happened,
- which steps depended on earlier steps,
- and how influential each contribution was.

The goal is **provenance, not AI detection**.

---

## Example

A real content workflow might look like this:

```text
Human
  │
  │ concept, expertise, facts
  ▼
Claude
  │
  │ research assistance
  ▼
GPT
  │
  │ structure, drafting, wording
  ▼
DeepL
  │
  │ translation
  ▼
Human
  │
  │ fact-checking, review, approval
  ▼
Published content
```

HACP represents this as structured, machine-readable metadata.

## Why another provenance specification?

A binary label such as:

```text
AI generated: true
```

loses important information.

For example, the following scenarios are materially different:

1. A human writes an article without generative AI.
2. A human provides the idea, expertise and facts while AI improves wording.
3. Human and AI jointly develop an argument and draft.
4. AI generates an article autonomously from a short prompt.
5. Several AI systems perform different stages such as research, drafting and translation.
6. AI produces a draft, followed by human fact-checking and final approval.

HACP describes these differences explicitly.

## Core concepts

### Systems

`systems` describes AI systems involved in the workflow.

It answers:

> Which AI providers, products or models participated?

### Workflow

`workflow` is the core of HACP.

It answers:

> What actually happened during content creation?

Each step can describe:

- an actor,
- contribution types,
- influence,
- sequence,
- dependencies.

### Influence

HACP intentionally avoids fake numerical precision such as:

```text
GPT: 63 %
Human: 27 %
Claude: 10 %
```

Authorship and intellectual influence are not reliably measurable as percentages.

Instead, HACP defines four qualitative influence levels:

- `minor`
- `supporting`
- `substantial`
- `primary`

### Dependencies

`dependsOn` allows workflows to represent more than a simple linear history.

Example:

```text
               ┌─ AI research ─────┐
Human concept ─┤                    ├─ AI draft
               └─ Human research ──┘
```

This allows HACP to represent a provenance graph rather than only a list.

## Relationship to C2PA

HACP is intended to **complement**, not replace, established provenance systems such as C2PA.

HACP focuses on:

- contribution semantics,
- human/AI collaboration,
- workflow stages,
- actor roles,
- influence.

Existing provenance frameworks can provide mechanisms such as:

- cryptographic content binding,
- signatures,
- signer identity,
- trust chains,
- validation.

See [`C2PA-MAPPING.md`](./C2PA-MAPPING.md).

## Trust model

A standalone HACP document is a **provenance declaration**.

It does not automatically prove that every claim is true.

For example:

```json
{
  "actor": {
    "type": "human"
  },
  "contributions": [
    "concept"
  ]
}
```

means that the issuer claims that the concept was contributed by a human.

Cryptographic attestations and external provenance systems may make such claims independently verifiable.

Future versions may support provider-specific attestations for individual workflow steps.

## Goals

HACP aims to be:

- open,
- vendor neutral,
- simple to implement,
- machine readable,
- human understandable,
- extensible,
- privacy preserving,
- compatible with existing provenance standards.

Potential consumers include:

- publishers,
- CMS platforms,
- search engines,
- browsers,
- AI providers,
- enterprise governance tools,
- content verification services,
- academic systems,
- archives.

---

## Non-goals

HACP is not intended to:

- infer whether content “looks AI-generated”,
- provide an AI detection score,
- determine whether content is true,
- determine whether content is good,
- penalize AI usage,
- define search-engine ranking behavior,
- replace plagiarism detection,
- replace cryptographic provenance standards.

## Status

HACP v0.2 is an experimental draft intended for discussion and implementation experiments.

The vocabulary and data model are expected to evolve.

The project name **HACP is a working name** and may change before a stable release.

---

## Commercial implementations

The specification is intended to remain openly implementable.

Commercial products and services may be built around the specification, including:

- verification services,
- issuer identity verification,
- signing infrastructure,
- attestation services,
- audit trails,
- provider analytics,
- enterprise policy engines,
- CMS integrations,
- compliance tooling.

The open specification and commercial services are deliberately separate concerns.
